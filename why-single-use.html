The Problem

Healthcare Associated Infections Cost Lives and Billions

Reusable surgical instruments require complex reprocessing cycles involving cleaning, disinfection, inspection, packaging, and sterilization. Each step introduces the potential for human error, and even a single failure can put patients at risk.

Despite best efforts, healthcare associated infections (HAIs) remain one of the most significant threats to patient safety in the United States.

1 in 31 Hospital patients has at least one HAI on any given day CDC, 2023
$28.4B Annual cost of HAIs to the US healthcare system
1.7M HAIs occur in US hospitals each year
99,000 Deaths attributed to HAIs annually
The Reprocessing Problem

Every Step Is a Risk

Reusable instruments must go through a complex, multi-step reprocessing chain after every use. Each step requires trained staff, specialized equipment, and rigorous quality control.

1

Collection & Transport

Used instruments collected from operating rooms and transported to the sterile processing department.

2

Manual Cleaning

Instruments must be disassembled and manually cleaned to remove all organic material before sterilization.

3

Inspection & Testing

Each instrument inspected for damage, corrosion, and cleanliness. Biological indicators tested.

4

Packaging & Sterilization

Wrapped, sealed, and run through autoclave cycles. Must be stored in controlled environments.

5

Storage & Retrieval

Sterile instruments stored, tracked, and retrieved for the next procedure. Expiration dates monitored.
